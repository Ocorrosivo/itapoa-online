# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Easy to find and reach (the one market bright spot the Offers lens rewards):** Hiperlocal by design — 22 bairros, 10 categories, ~80k city means the buyer is physically findable door-to-door and on WhatsApp. That lowers effort to test an offer fast, even if the market is small.
- **Low cost to test the offer:** Product is live in production (React 19 + Vite 6 + Express 4/MariaDB 11, `itapoa.online` canonical, Mercado Pago webhook as single `paid` path, 37 tables, 16 demo listings). Incremental cost of running a paid pilot is small, so the value equation can be tested without media spend.
- **Stack has the raw ingredients for an outcome, not just a listing:** destaque/busca/mini-site/CRM/vagas maps to the actual path to the outcome (be found → be understood → capture lead → hire/close). If rebundled around a promised outcome instead of features, there is something to stack with bonuses and a guarantee.
- **Price lowers sacrifice, which helps the value equation denominator:** R$ 0 / R$ 49,90 / R$ 99,90 is low effort to say yes for a first month. That is not a moat, but it makes a short, proof-based pilot easier to sell while likelihood is built.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Market is small, seasonal, and pain is not yet proven as massive — the Offers lens fails here first.** Itapoá ~80k hab. with tourism seasonality and free substitutes that already solve "be found" (Google Perfil da Empresa / Maps, Instagram, WhatsApp Business, OLX local, prefeitura/turismo portals). If pain were massive, buyers would already pay > R$ 99,90 for discovery; no evidence of that yet. TAM of businesses willing to pay monthly is an open question — needs `competitors` + `consumer` to size. The Offers lens says a weak market is the most common reason to pass, and this is that risk.
2. **Perceived likelihood of achieving the outcome is near zero + time to value is long.** Value equation: dream outcome (more local customers) is vague and undifferentiated from Google; perceived likelihood collapses with zero paying proof (only 16 demo companies, no case studies, no measured leads); time delay is high because SEO/local search needs traffic before it delivers leads — classic chicken-egg. Most offers fail on likelihood or effort, and this fails on likelihood. Until a buyer sees "X leads in Y days," the offer feels like another directory.
3. **Competing on price as a commodity with no "feel stupid saying no" stack.** Free bronze vs. R$ 49,90 vs. R$ 99,90 with feature-list positioning (destaque, mini-site, CRM, vagas) gives no reason to choose this over free alternatives. No named offer, no scarcity/urgency (real, not fake), no bonus, no guarantee. At these prices, doubling the price would be impossible today — which the Offers lens uses as the test for whether the offer is actually strong.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly has both the pain and the money to pay monthly in Itapoá? Of the 10 categories, which 2 segments would you bet on for gold_vip vs. diamond_extremo, and why now? What do they currently pay for Google/Instagram/WhatsApp to get the same outcome?
- Why would a buyer believe this works? What is the proof asset — 1 paying case with measured leads, calls, or hires — and how fast can you produce 3–5 of them?
- How fast to first value? From payment to first qualified lead/contact, in days — and what do you push on day 1–7 that is not "wait for SEO"? Time delay is the silent killer here.
- What is the guarantee you can afford to make? If a gold_vip does not get Y contacts in 30 days, what happens — and what does that cost you? (needs `cfo` to model)
- Could you double the price to R$ 99,90 → R$ 199 and still be the obvious choice? What would you have to add to the stack (done-for-you, not just access) to make that true?
- What is the stack, problem-by-problem? List every problem the buyer hits on the way to "new customer" and which stack element solves it cheaply for you but valuably for them. Which features would you cut?
- Open numbers I will not invent — which skill answers each: total addressable paying businesses in Itapoá and their current spend (`competitors` + `consumer`), CAC by channel (door-to-door, WhatsApp, SEO) (`consumer` + `cfo`), LTV/churn at R$ 49,90 and R$ 99,90 (`cfo`), leads per paying customer per month and conversion to sale (`consumer`).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Fund a narrow, proof-first pilot only — do not invest in media until these are met. Kill or rework if not met in 45–60 days.

1. **Prove likelihood with paid pilots before any media:** Close 10–15 paying customers across 2 focused segments (not all 10 categories) at current prices, with written permission to publish results. Measure and report: views → contacts/leads → sales/hires per customer per month. No new feature work until this exists.
2. **Rebuild the offer as an outcome stack with a name, bonuses, and a real guarantee:** Rename the paid offers around the outcome (not "gold/diamond"), stack only the problems that move the value equation (e.g., done-for-you mini-site + photo help, WhatsApp lead routing, ranking boost), add 1–2 cheap-to-deliver bonuses, and add a guarantee the `cfo` has priced (e.g., "Y contacts in 30 days or next month free / done-for-you push"). Add real scarcity/urgency only if true (e.g., limited slots per bairro/category).
3. **Fix time delay to < 7 days to first lead:** Ship a day-1 activation play that does not depend on SEO — e.g., featured placement + WhatsApp broadcast to existing audience + direct push in search — so every new payer gets a tangible result in week one. Track "days to first qualified contact."
4. **Do not compete on price — pass the price-doubling test:** Define what must be true to charge 2× and still be the obvious choice. If you cannot name the stack that justifies it, cut scope until you can, rather than discounting.
5. **Answer the open numbers with the right skills before scaling:** `competitors` maps who already charges for the same outcome locally and at what price; `consumer` validates willingness to pay and CAC by channel with 15–20 buyer interviews; `cfo` models LTV, churn, and guarantee cost at R$ 49,90 / R$ 99,90 (and 2×) to set a hard CAC ceiling. No scale spend approved until CAC < 1/3 LTV is shown on pilot data.

If these conditions are not met, the Offers lens would move to PASS — the market/offer fit is not there at a price that can support a business, and further build would burn into a commodity directory competing with free.
